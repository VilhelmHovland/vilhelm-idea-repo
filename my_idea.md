Tesseract is an open-source OCR (Optical Character Recognition) tool. OCR tools take images containing text as input and  outputs text in machine-readable form after somehow identifying the text in the image. Tesseract contains its own OCR engine as well as a command line interface. The OCR engine is built using a neural network architecture and is trained for English, but the repository includes instructions and programs for adapting the engine to other languages with new training data. Tesseract was initially developed between 1985 and 1994 by Hewlett-Packard. It was open-sourced in 2005 and developed by Google from 2006 to 2018 and is now developed and maintained independently.

My field is Intellectual History and Knowledge History which largely concerns itself with analysis of historical text. For "distant reading" and quantitative analysis of corpora of historical text it is necessary to convert this text into machine readable form. Historical text is often very challenging for OCR systems to handle as these texts typically have archaic scripts or are handwritten, they can be structurally messy, and be incomplete or damaged.
